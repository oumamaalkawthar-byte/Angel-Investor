Being "investment-ready" isn't a single milestone — it's a combination of evidence, discipline and clarity that lets an investor evaluate the opportunity quickly instead of guessing.

Before the first serious conversation, a founder should be able to answer three questions without hesitation: who is the customer, what evidence shows they'll pay, and what will the capital actually change. Vague answers here are usually a sign the business itself is still vague.

Investors also read discipline in how a founder tracks their own numbers. A team that knows its burn rate, retention and unit economics cold — without needing to check a spreadsheet mid-conversation — signals a business that's actually being run, not just pitched.
