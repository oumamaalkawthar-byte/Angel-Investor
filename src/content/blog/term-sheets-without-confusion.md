A term sheet is short by design, but every line in it has downstream consequences that are easy to miss on a first read. Liquidation preference, anti-dilution protection and board composition are the three clauses worth the most attention, because they shape outcomes years after signing — not just at the moment of the raise.

Liquidation preference decides who gets paid first, and how much, in an exit. A 1x non-participating preference is standard and founder-friendly; anything stacked or participating compounds quickly across multiple rounds.

None of this requires a founder to become a lawyer. It requires knowing which three or four terms are worth a real conversation with counsel, versus which are standard boilerplate not worth negotiating over.
