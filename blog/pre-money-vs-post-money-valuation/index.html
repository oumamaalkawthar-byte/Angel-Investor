Pre-money vs post-money valuation, explained clearly

Pre-money valuation is what a company is worth right before new investment comes in. Post-money is that same number plus the new capital raised. The gap between the two is exactly the size of the round — and it’s the single most common source of confusion in a term sheet conversation.

The distinction matters most when it comes to ownership. If an investor’s cheque is priced against the pre-money figure, the resulting equity stake will be larger than if it’s priced against post-money — same cheque size, different dilution for the founder.

A useful habit: whenever a valuation number comes up in conversation, ask explicitly whether it’s pre- or post-money before doing any mental math on ownership. It’s a two-second question that avoids a much longer disagreement later.
